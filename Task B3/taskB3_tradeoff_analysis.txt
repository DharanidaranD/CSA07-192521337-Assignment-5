TASK B3 - TRADE-OFF (complete after you have measured)

Use these theory-based conclusions as hypotheses to confirm or refute with your numbers:
 * Delay: TCP with a fixed 64 KB window loses throughput in proportion to RTT (10.5 -> 3.5 -> 1.75 Mbps at 50/150/300 ms).
   Voice depends on one-way delay budget (150 ms): 300 ms of added delay makes interactive voice unusable, regardless of bandwidth.
 * Loss: TCP throughput ~ 1.22 x MSS / (RTT x sqrt(p)): at 1/5/10 % loss and 50 ms RTT = 2.9 / 1.3 / 0.9 Mbps (theory), i.e. loss hurts TCP
   much more than a small delay; voice tolerates ~1% (with concealment), degrades at 5%, unusable at 10%.
 * Window: bigger windows (window scaling, ~BDP) restore throughput on long-delay links; do not help when loss is the limit.
 * Firewall + rate limit: expect the corrected rules to block outside SSH while 443 is unaffected; rate limiting should keep
   response time near the no-flood value while the unprotected server shows large response times/failures.
Best settings per application (confirm with data):
   Live voice/video: lowest delay & jitter, loss < 1%, strict priority (EF), UDP/RTP, regional server.
   Downloads/recorded video: large TCP window, CDN, can tolerate delay, best-effort/WFQ class.
   E-mail: anything works; reliability (TCP) matters, not delay.
Alternative (strict priority vs WFQ): expected - strict priority gives voice the lowest delay (waits one packet) but can starve
downloads if voice traffic is unbounded (so police it, e.g. 30% of link); WFQ shares bandwidth fairly, giving voice more delay
(depends on weights) but never starves anyone. Recommendation unless your data show otherwise: priority queue for voice limited to a
cap + WFQ for the rest (Cisco LLQ).
