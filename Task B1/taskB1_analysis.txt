ALTERNATIVE - one-way delay vs RTT/2 on an asymmetric path
 Example: download 100 Mbps, upload 2 Mbps, 1,500 B packets, 10 ms propagation each way.
 Upstream transmission = 12,000 bit / 2 Mbps = 6 ms; downstream = 12,000 / 100 Mbps = 0.12 ms.
 If the upload link also has a 40 ms queue (bufferbloat while uploading): 
   True one-way delay up   = 10 + 6 + 40 = 56 ms ; down = 10 + 0.12 = 10.12 ms; RTT = 66.1 ms; RTT/2 = 33 ms.
 RTT/2 under-states the upstream delay by 23 ms (41%) and over-states the downstream delay by 23 ms (3x).
 For a voice call student->teacher, the real delay is 56 ms, not 33 ms. One-way delay measurement needs synchronised 
 clocks (GPS/PTP; NTP error of a few ms is acceptable only if the delay is tens of ms).
